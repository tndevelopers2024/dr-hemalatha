Tests & scans in pregnancy

Every test explained, and planned around you

Pregnancy involves many tests and scans. Here is what each one checks, when it usually happens, and how Dr. Hemalatha decides what you need.

Our approach to tests

  • No tests for the sake of tests: each one has a reason
  • Chosen for your age, history and risk
  • Every report reviewed by Dr. Hemalatha herself
  • Results explained in simple words, the same day
  • Extra monitoring only when you need it

Pregnancy confirmation

At your missed period

A urine or blood test (hCG) confirms that you are pregnant.

For youWe confirm early so your care, folic acid and diet start on time.

Dating & viability scan

Weeks 6–9

An early ultrasound that shows the baby's heartbeat, checks the pregnancy is in the womb, and sets your due date. It also shows twins.

For youIf you have had pain, bleeding or a past miscarriage, we may do it earlier for reassurance.

First blood tests

First visit

Blood group and Rh type, haemoglobin (for anaemia), blood sugar, thyroid, and infection screening (HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis), plus a urine test.

For youResults are explained to you the same day, and any problem, like low thyroid, is treated straight away.

NT scan + double marker

Weeks 11–13

The NT scan measures a small fluid space behind the baby's neck. With a blood test, it screens for conditions such as Down syndrome.

For youWe explain what a screening result means and does not mean, and discuss next steps calmly if more testing is advised.

NIPT (optional)

From week 10

An advanced blood test that screens the baby's DNA from the mother's blood, with higher accuracy.

For youOffered when your age or earlier results suggest it, never pushed on everyone.

Anomaly scan (TIFFA / Level II)

Weeks 18–22

A detailed scan that checks the baby's brain, heart, spine, kidneys, limbs and the placenta.

For youDr. Hemalatha reviews every report herself and walks you through it in simple words.

Quadruple marker

Weeks 15–20

A blood screening test for some chromosomal conditions, if the NT scan was missed.

For youOnly advised if you did not have first-trimester screening.

Glucose tolerance test (OGTT)

Weeks 24–28

You drink a glucose solution and give blood samples to check for diabetes in pregnancy.

For youDone earlier if you have a family history, high weight or past gestational diabetes.

Fetal echo (if needed)

Weeks 20–24

A focused scan of the baby's heart.

For youAdvised only when there is a specific reason, such as diabetes or a family history of heart problems.

Haemoglobin & vaccines

Around week 20–28

Repeat blood count to check for anaemia, and recommended vaccines such as Tdap.

For youIron and diet are adjusted to your results, not a one-size plan.

Growth scan + Doppler

Weeks 28–36

Checks the baby's size, position, fluid around the baby, and blood flow through the cord and placenta.

For youHow often you need it depends on your pregnancy: more often if high-risk, less if all is well.

NST / CTG

From week 32–36, as needed

A painless monitor on your tummy records the baby's heartbeat and movements for 20–30 minutes.

For youUsed when you feel fewer movements, are past your due date, or have a high-risk pregnancy.

Repeat blood & urine tests

Around week 32–36

Haemoglobin, urine and other tests to prepare safely for delivery.

For youHelps plan your delivery and pain relief, such as an epidural, safely.

Biophysical profile (if needed)

Late pregnancy

A scan that scores baby's breathing movements, body movements, muscle tone and fluid.

For youOnly when extra reassurance about baby's well-being is needed.

Timings are a general guide. Dr. Hemalatha will advise the tests that are right for your pregnancy.

Planning a pregnancy

  • Visit a doctor 3 months before trying to conceive.
  • Start folic acid tablets before pregnancy.
  • Check blood sugar, thyroid, blood count and vaccinations.
  • Healthy weight, no smoking or alcohol.

Healthy pregnancy tips

  • Food — home-cooked meals, fruits, vegetables, dal, milk, eggs or other protein. Avoid raw or undercooked food.
  • Water — drink plenty through the day.
  • Activity — walking and gentle exercise, unless advised otherwise.
  • Rest — sleep on your left side in later months.
  • Medicines — take nothing without asking your doctor.

Warning signs — call your doctor immediately

Bleeding from the vagina · severe pain in the tummy · baby moving less than usual · leaking of water · severe headache, blurred vision, or swelling of face and hands · high fever · fits (seizures).

Hospital bag checklist

For you — ID and medical file, loose comfortable clothes, sanitary pads, toiletries, slippers.

For baby — soft clothes, cap, socks, mittens, swaddle cloth, diapers.

After the birth

  • Rest, eat well and accept help.
  • Breastfeed on demand — ask for help if it is difficult.
  • Watch for heavy bleeding, fever, or severe sadness, and call us.

Women's health basics

When period pain is not normal — pain that stops daily life, needs strong painkillers every month, or is getting worse.

When to see a gynaecologist — irregular or very heavy periods, pelvic pain, pain during intercourse, or difficulty conceiving.
