Pregnancy Care — with you through all nine months

Dr. HemalathaYour doctor at every visitBook a consultation →

What is it?

Antenatal care means the regular check-ups you have while you are pregnant. They make sure you and your baby are healthy and growing well, and help catch any problem early.

Who is it for?

Every pregnant woman — from the day the pregnancy test is positive. Couples planning a pregnancy can also visit for a pre-pregnancy check.

What happens during your pregnancy

  • Check-ups — about once a month at first, every two weeks after 28 weeks, and every week from 36 weeks.
  • Tests — blood tests, urine tests, blood pressure and sugar checks.
  • Scans — dating scan, NT scan (around 11–13 weeks), anomaly scan (around 18–22 weeks) and growth scans later.
  • Vaccines and supplements — folic acid, iron, calcium and recommended vaccines.
  • Guidance — food, exercise, sleep, travel, work and what to avoid.
  • Birth preparation — your birth plan, labour signs and pain-relief choices.
Personalised care

How we personalise your care

  • Dr. Hemalatha sees you at every visit — no change of doctors.
  • Your diet, tests and scans are planned around your age, health, history and lifestyle.
  • Your husband and family are welcome to join consultations and ask questions.
  • You get a simple written plan of upcoming visits and tests.
  • Your emotional health matters — anxiety, low mood and fears are discussed openly.

Call us right away if you have

Bleeding · severe tummy pain · baby moving less than usual · water leaking · severe headache or blurred vision · sudden swelling of face or hands · fever.

Common questions

When should I first visit?

As soon as your pregnancy test is positive, ideally before 8 weeks.

Is exercise safe?

For most women, gentle walking and prenatal yoga are safe. Dr. Hemalatha will advise you based on your health.

How many scans will I need?

Usually 3–4 for a normal pregnancy; more if extra monitoring is needed.

Tests & scans in pregnancy

Every test explained, and planned around you

Pregnancy involves many tests and scans. Here is what each one checks, when it usually happens, and how Dr. Hemalatha decides what you need.

Our approach to tests

  • No tests for the sake of tests: each one has a reason
  • Chosen for your age, history and risk
  • Every report reviewed by Dr. Hemalatha herself
  • Results explained in simple words, the same day
  • Extra monitoring only when you need it

Pregnancy confirmation

At your missed period

A urine or blood test (hCG) confirms that you are pregnant.

For youWe confirm early so your care, folic acid and diet start on time.

Dating & viability scan

Weeks 6–9

An early ultrasound that shows the baby's heartbeat, checks the pregnancy is in the womb, and sets your due date. It also shows twins.

For youIf you have had pain, bleeding or a past miscarriage, we may do it earlier for reassurance.

First blood tests

First visit

Blood group and Rh type, haemoglobin (for anaemia), blood sugar, thyroid, and infection screening (HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis), plus a urine test.

For youResults are explained to you the same day, and any problem, like low thyroid, is treated straight away.

NT scan + double marker

Weeks 11–13

The NT scan measures a small fluid space behind the baby's neck. With a blood test, it screens for conditions such as Down syndrome.

For youWe explain what a screening result means and does not mean, and discuss next steps calmly if more testing is advised.

NIPT (optional)

From week 10

An advanced blood test that screens the baby's DNA from the mother's blood, with higher accuracy.

For youOffered when your age or earlier results suggest it, never pushed on everyone.

Anomaly scan (TIFFA / Level II)

Weeks 18–22

A detailed scan that checks the baby's brain, heart, spine, kidneys, limbs and the placenta.

For youDr. Hemalatha reviews every report herself and walks you through it in simple words.

Quadruple marker

Weeks 15–20

A blood screening test for some chromosomal conditions, if the NT scan was missed.

For youOnly advised if you did not have first-trimester screening.

Glucose tolerance test (OGTT)

Weeks 24–28

You drink a glucose solution and give blood samples to check for diabetes in pregnancy.

For youDone earlier if you have a family history, high weight or past gestational diabetes.

Fetal echo (if needed)

Weeks 20–24

A focused scan of the baby's heart.

For youAdvised only when there is a specific reason, such as diabetes or a family history of heart problems.

Haemoglobin & vaccines

Around week 20–28

Repeat blood count to check for anaemia, and recommended vaccines such as Tdap.

For youIron and diet are adjusted to your results, not a one-size plan.

Growth scan + Doppler

Weeks 28–36

Checks the baby's size, position, fluid around the baby, and blood flow through the cord and placenta.

For youHow often you need it depends on your pregnancy: more often if high-risk, less if all is well.

NST / CTG

From week 32–36, as needed

A painless monitor on your tummy records the baby's heartbeat and movements for 20–30 minutes.

For youUsed when you feel fewer movements, are past your due date, or have a high-risk pregnancy.

Repeat blood & urine tests

Around week 32–36

Haemoglobin, urine and other tests to prepare safely for delivery.

For youHelps plan your delivery and pain relief, such as an epidural, safely.

Biophysical profile (if needed)

Late pregnancy

A scan that scores baby's breathing movements, body movements, muscle tone and fluid.

For youOnly when extra reassurance about baby's well-being is needed.

Timings are a general guide. Dr. Hemalatha will advise the tests that are right for your pregnancy.

High-risk pregnancy — closer care, the same personal attention

Dr. HemalathaYour doctor at every visitBook a consultation →

What is it?

A pregnancy is called “high-risk” when the mother or baby needs extra attention to stay safe. It does not mean something will go wrong. It means you need closer monitoring — and with the right care, most high-risk pregnancies end with a healthy mother and baby.

Who may need it?

  • Mothers above 35 or below 18 years
  • High blood pressure or pre-eclampsia
  • Diabetes before or during pregnancy
  • Thyroid problems
  • Twins or triplets
  • Previous C-section, miscarriages or early births
  • Anaemia (low blood)
  • Problems with the placenta or baby's growth
  • Pregnancy after IVF or fertility treatment

How we work with high-risk pregnancies

  1. Detailed first assessment — your full history, tests and risks reviewed in one unhurried visit.
  2. Your personal monitoring plan — extra check-ups, scans, BP and sugar monitoring, planned for your specific risk.
  3. A team of experts around you — fetal medicine experts, physicians, diabetologists, cardiologists, anaesthetists and neonatologists (baby doctors).
  4. One doctor leading your care — even with many experts, Dr. Hemalatha remains your main doctor and coordinates everything.
  5. Planned, safe delivery — timing and type of delivery planned in advance, in a hospital equipped for emergencies and newborn intensive care.
Personalised care

How we personalise your care

  • Every risk is explained clearly — what it means, what we watch for, and what you can do.
  • Monitoring is matched to your risk level — not more, not less.
  • Quick access to the doctor for any warning sign.
  • Emotional support for you and your family through an anxious time.

A safe, calm birth — with your doctor by your side

Dr. HemalathaYour doctor at every visitBook a consultation →

What is it?

Labour is the process of your body getting ready to deliver your baby. Delivery can be a normal (vaginal) delivery or a Caesarean section (C-section) when it is safer for you or your baby.

Our approach

  • We support normal delivery whenever it is safe.
  • A C-section is advised only when there is a clear medical reason, and the reason is explained to you.
  • Dr. Hemalatha personally attends your delivery.

Signs that labour has started

  • Regular, painful tightening of the tummy that comes closer together
  • Water breaking — a gush or steady leak of fluid
  • A “show” — sticky, slightly blood-stained discharge

What happens at the hospital

  1. Check-up of you and baby's heartbeat
  2. Monitoring as labour progresses
  3. Pain relief, including epidural if you choose
  4. Delivery, with baby on your chest when possible
  5. Newborn check by the baby doctor
  6. Support to start breastfeeding
Personalised care

How we personalise your care

  • Your birth plan is discussed and written down before your due date.
  • Your preferences — who stays with you, pain relief, feeding — are respected wherever safely possible.
  • The doctor who knows your whole pregnancy is the one who delivers your baby.

After delivery

Recovery check-ups, stitches care, breastfeeding help, and guidance for your baby's first weeks.

Painless labour — give birth with comfort and confidence

Dr. HemalathaYour doctor at every visitBook a consultation →

What is it?

Painless labour means using an epidural — a safe, common method of pain relief during labour. A very thin, soft tube is placed in your lower back by an anaesthetist (a pain-relief specialist). Medicine flows through it and numbs the pain from your waist down.

What it feels like

  • You stay fully awake and aware.
  • You still feel pressure and know when to push, but the sharp pain is greatly reduced.
  • You can rest during labour and be calmer for the birth.

Possible side effects (usually mild)

  • Drop in blood pressure (monitored closely)
  • Itching or shivering
  • Soreness where the tube was placed
  • Rarely, headache

Who can have it?

Most women in labour can choose an epidural. It may not be suitable with certain bleeding problems, a back infection, or some spine conditions — Dr. Hemalatha will check this during your pregnancy.

Is it safe for my baby?

For most mothers and babies an epidural is considered safe. Only a small amount of medicine reaches the baby.

Personalised care

How we personalise your care

  • We discuss pain relief before your due date, so you can decide without pressure.
  • You can choose an epidural, other pain relief, or none — it is your choice.
  • An experienced anaesthetist and Dr. Hemalatha monitor you throughout.

Common questions

Will an epidural slow down my labour?

It may slightly lengthen the pushing stage; your team will guide you.

Can I still have a normal delivery?

Yes. Most women with an epidural have a normal delivery.

When can I ask for it?

Usually once active labour has started. Your doctor will advise the right time.

Endometriosis — your pain is real, and it can be treated

Treated by Dr. Hemalatha, Certified Endometriosis Surgeon.

Dr. HemalathaYour doctor at every visitBook a consultation →

What is it?

Endometriosis is a condition where tissue similar to the lining of the womb grows outside the womb — on the ovaries, tubes or other parts of the pelvis. Every month this tissue reacts to periods, causing pain, swelling and sometimes scarring.

Many women live with it for years, thinking severe period pain is “normal”. It is not — and help is available.

Common signs

  • Very painful periods that stop daily work
  • Pain during or after intercourse
  • Long-lasting pain in the lower tummy or back
  • Pain passing urine or stools during periods
  • Heavy or irregular periods
  • Difficulty in getting pregnant
  • Tiredness, bloating

How it is found

A detailed talk about your symptoms, a pelvic examination and an ultrasound or MRI scan. In some cases, laparoscopy is used to confirm it.

Treatment options — chosen for you

There is no single treatment for every woman. The right choice depends on your symptoms, your age and whether you plan to get pregnant.

1. Medical management

Pain-relief medicines and hormone treatment to calm the endometriosis and reduce periods or pain. Best for milder symptoms, women not ready for surgery, or to control symptoms after surgery.

2. Surgical management

Advised when medicines do not help enough, when there are cysts (endometriomas), or when it is affecting fertility.

3. Laparoscopic endometriosis excision surgery

The endometriosis tissue is carefully cut out and removed through keyhole surgery, rather than only burnt on the surface. Removing the disease can give longer-lasting relief and helps protect fertility. It aims to remove the disease while protecting healthy organs, and is often combined with medical treatment afterwards.

Personalised care

How we personalise your care

  • Your pain is taken seriously from the first visit.
  • Your treatment plan considers your symptoms, your age and your plans for a family.
  • Medicines are tried first where suitable; surgery is advised only when it helps you more.
  • Long-term follow-up with the same doctor, to keep symptoms under control.
  • If you wish to become pregnant, your treatment is planned to protect your fertility.

Common questions

Can endometriosis be cured?

It can be well controlled. Many women become pain-free or have far less pain after the right treatment.

Can I get pregnant with endometriosis?

Yes, many women do. Treatment can improve the chances.

Will it come back after surgery?

It can return in some women, which is why regular follow-up matters.

Laparoscopy is surgery done through 2–4 tiny cuts (about 0.5–1 cm) in the tummy, instead of one large cut. A thin camera shows the inside of the body on a screen, and the surgeon uses fine instruments to treat the problem.

Trying for a baby? Let's find the way together.

Dr. HemalathaYour doctor at every visitBook a consultation →

What is it?

Fertility care helps couples who are finding it hard to get pregnant. Doctors usually suggest a check-up if you have been trying for 12 months, or 6 months if the woman is over 35.

Common causes we look for

  • Irregular or no ovulation (for example, PCOS)
  • Blocked tubes
  • Endometriosis
  • Fibroids or problems in the womb
  • Thyroid or hormone problems
  • Sperm-related issues in the husband

How we help

  1. Check-up for both partners — history, scans, blood tests and semen test.
  2. Treating the cause — medicines, lifestyle changes, or keyhole surgery for endometriosis, cysts or blocked tubes.
  3. Fertility treatment — tablets or injections to help ovulation, timed intercourse, IUI, and guidance on IVF when needed.

The endometriosis link

Endometriosis is a common hidden reason for difficulty in conceiving. As a certified endometriosis surgeon, Dr. Hemalatha can treat it while protecting your fertility.

Personalised care

How we personalise your care

  • Private, unhurried consultations for the couple.
  • A step-by-step plan explained clearly, starting with the simplest options.
  • Emotional support through a stressful journey.

Tests & scans in pregnancy

Every test explained, and planned around you

Pregnancy involves many tests and scans. Here is what each one checks, when it usually happens, and how Dr. Hemalatha decides what you need.

Our approach to tests

  • No tests for the sake of tests: each one has a reason
  • Chosen for your age, history and risk
  • Every report reviewed by Dr. Hemalatha herself
  • Results explained in simple words, the same day
  • Extra monitoring only when you need it

Pregnancy confirmation

At your missed period

A urine or blood test (hCG) confirms that you are pregnant.

For youWe confirm early so your care, folic acid and diet start on time.

Dating & viability scan

Weeks 6–9

An early ultrasound that shows the baby's heartbeat, checks the pregnancy is in the womb, and sets your due date. It also shows twins.

For youIf you have had pain, bleeding or a past miscarriage, we may do it earlier for reassurance.

First blood tests

First visit

Blood group and Rh type, haemoglobin (for anaemia), blood sugar, thyroid, and infection screening (HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis), plus a urine test.

For youResults are explained to you the same day, and any problem, like low thyroid, is treated straight away.

NT scan + double marker

Weeks 11–13

The NT scan measures a small fluid space behind the baby's neck. With a blood test, it screens for conditions such as Down syndrome.

For youWe explain what a screening result means and does not mean, and discuss next steps calmly if more testing is advised.

NIPT (optional)

From week 10

An advanced blood test that screens the baby's DNA from the mother's blood, with higher accuracy.

For youOffered when your age or earlier results suggest it, never pushed on everyone.

Anomaly scan (TIFFA / Level II)

Weeks 18–22

A detailed scan that checks the baby's brain, heart, spine, kidneys, limbs and the placenta.

For youDr. Hemalatha reviews every report herself and walks you through it in simple words.

Quadruple marker

Weeks 15–20

A blood screening test for some chromosomal conditions, if the NT scan was missed.

For youOnly advised if you did not have first-trimester screening.

Glucose tolerance test (OGTT)

Weeks 24–28

You drink a glucose solution and give blood samples to check for diabetes in pregnancy.

For youDone earlier if you have a family history, high weight or past gestational diabetes.

Fetal echo (if needed)

Weeks 20–24

A focused scan of the baby's heart.

For youAdvised only when there is a specific reason, such as diabetes or a family history of heart problems.

Haemoglobin & vaccines

Around week 20–28

Repeat blood count to check for anaemia, and recommended vaccines such as Tdap.

For youIron and diet are adjusted to your results, not a one-size plan.

Growth scan + Doppler

Weeks 28–36

Checks the baby's size, position, fluid around the baby, and blood flow through the cord and placenta.

For youHow often you need it depends on your pregnancy: more often if high-risk, less if all is well.

NST / CTG

From week 32–36, as needed

A painless monitor on your tummy records the baby's heartbeat and movements for 20–30 minutes.

For youUsed when you feel fewer movements, are past your due date, or have a high-risk pregnancy.

Repeat blood & urine tests

Around week 32–36

Haemoglobin, urine and other tests to prepare safely for delivery.

For youHelps plan your delivery and pain relief, such as an epidural, safely.

Biophysical profile (if needed)

Late pregnancy

A scan that scores baby's breathing movements, body movements, muscle tone and fluid.

For youOnly when extra reassurance about baby's well-being is needed.

Timings are a general guide. Dr. Hemalatha will advise the tests that are right for your pregnancy.

Planning a pregnancy

  • Visit a doctor 3 months before trying to conceive.
  • Start folic acid tablets before pregnancy.
  • Check blood sugar, thyroid, blood count and vaccinations.
  • Healthy weight, no smoking or alcohol.

Healthy pregnancy tips

  • Food — home-cooked meals, fruits, vegetables, dal, milk, eggs or other protein. Avoid raw or undercooked food.
  • Water — drink plenty through the day.
  • Activity — walking and gentle exercise, unless advised otherwise.
  • Rest — sleep on your left side in later months.
  • Medicines — take nothing without asking your doctor.

Warning signs — call your doctor immediately

Bleeding from the vagina · severe pain in the tummy · baby moving less than usual · leaking of water · severe headache, blurred vision, or swelling of face and hands · high fever · fits (seizures).

Hospital bag checklist

For you — ID and medical file, loose comfortable clothes, sanitary pads, toiletries, slippers.

For baby — soft clothes, cap, socks, mittens, swaddle cloth, diapers.

After the birth

  • Rest, eat well and accept help.
  • Breastfeed on demand — ask for help if it is difficult.
  • Watch for heavy bleeding, fever, or severe sadness, and call us.

Women's health basics

When period pain is not normal — pain that stops daily life, needs strong painkillers every month, or is getting worse.

When to see a gynaecologist — irregular or very heavy periods, pelvic pain, pain during intercourse, or difficulty conceiving.
